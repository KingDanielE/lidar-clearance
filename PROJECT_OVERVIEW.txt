===============================================================================
1. THE PROBLEM IN ONE PARAGRAPH
===============================================================================

An overhead power line must keep a minimum distance from trees. Trees grow, and
the conductors move: on a hot day a conductor expands and sags lower, under ice
it is heavier and sags lower, and in a strong wind it swings sideways. A tree
that is clear of the line today, as it hangs during a survey, can be too close
on the worst day of the year. This project takes a raw LiDAR point cloud of a
power-line corridor (millions of 3D points with no labels) and answers, for
every tree: how close is it to a conductor now, how close would it be in the
worst-case hot, iced and windy conditions, is that closer than the law
allows, and could the tree hit the line if it fell. It reports the answer as
tiered "encroachment zones", a list of hazard trees, and an explicit list of
places it could not assess.


===============================================================================
2. WHY IT MATTERS AND WHAT A USEFUL ANSWER LOOKS LIKE
===============================================================================

Vegetation contact is a leading cause of power outages and can start wildfires.
Utilities therefore inspect corridors and trim trees on a cycle. LiDAR (laser
scanning from a drone, helicopter or aircraft) captures the corridor in 3D, but
a raw point cloud does not say which points are wires, which are trees, where
the conductors would be on a hot day, or which trees break the rules.

A useful answer must be:

  - Located: where exactly is the problem (coordinates, span, conductor).
  - Prioritised: what needs trimming now, soon, or just watching.
  - Physically right: based on where the conductor will be in the worst case,
    not only where it hung on the survey day.
  - Rule-based: tied to the legal clearance distance, not an arbitrary number.
  - Honest about gaps: if part of the line could not be modelled, the trees
    beside it must be reported as "not assessed", never as "clear". A silent
    gap is the most dangerous kind of error, because it looks like good news.
  - Verified: every number checked against independent ground truth.


===============================================================================
3. DESIGN PRINCIPLES
===============================================================================

  - No trained model. Every decision is a geometric rule or a physical equation
    that can be read in the code and explained. This makes the pipeline
    transparent, needs no training data, and fails in ways that can be traced.
  - Physics where physics applies. Conductors hang as catenaries; their
    behaviour under heat, ice and wind follows the conductor change-of-state
    equation. These are used directly rather than learned.
  - Evidence for every object. A tower must be supported by evidence (steel, a
    kink or turn in the wires, a line ending) and, after fitting, by a conductor
    actually hanging from it. A conductor must be long enough, well covered by
    points, smooth, and anchored.
  - Model only what the data supports. A conductor is modelled only over the
    stretch its points cover; it is not extrapolated to a tower it never
    reaches.
  - Measure end to end against independent truth, and record held-out tests as
    they come out, including failures.
  - Every rule carries the reason it exists. In the code and the docs, each
    threshold is accompanied by the real case that motivated it.


===============================================================================
4. THE DATA
===============================================================================

GridNet-HD (HEIG-VD, Switzerland; Carreaud et al., arXiv:2601.13052, 2026) is a
dataset of UAV (drone) LiDAR and imagery of high-voltage lines, with a label on
every point. Labels include several kinds of ground (grass, rock, road),
vegetation, buildings, the parts of a pylon (tower body, insulators, etc.) and
the wires (conductors and structural cables). This project maps them to five
classes, using ASPRS LAS codes where one exists:

    ground      (ASPRS 2)   grass, rock, road and other ground labels
    vegetation  (ASPRS 5)   trees and shrubs
    building    (ASPRS 6)
    pylon       (ASPRS 15)  tower body, insulators and other tower parts
    wire        (ASPRS 14)  conductors and structural cables

Only train and validation zones carry labels (test-zone labels are withheld).
The tiles used:

    tile    points    extent (x by y by relief)   character
    t5c2    30.6 M    268 x 581 m, 236 m          alpine; tuning tile
    t5b6    36.1 M    328 x 747 x 223 m           alpine; bigger towers
    t1z6a   50.3 M    213 x 610 x 91 m            forest; trees as tall as towers
    t1z6b   51.5 M    238 x 614 x 105 m           forest; neighbour of t1z6a
    t1z8    50.6 M    216 x 683 x 83 m            forest; first held-out test
    t2z5    66.7 M    515 x 680 x 162 m           a line junction
    t4z1   102.8 M    260 x 934 x 375 m           forest, steep; second held-out
    total  ~389 M points

Files are 1.2-4.3 GB each, downloaded from Hugging Face. The data is not in the
repository; the figures in docs/ and on the project page are derived from it
(CC BY 4.0, credited).

5. THE PIPELINE AT A GLANCE
===============================================================================

    LAS point cloud
        |
        v
    PHASE 1  classify:  ground / vegetation / wire / pylon / building
        |                (geometry rules on 0.15 m voxels)
        v
    PHASE 2  model the line:
        |      towers  ->  spans (a graph)  ->  conductors  ->  catenary fits
        v
    PHASE 3  clearance:
               each conductor as surveyed, hot (40 C), iced (0 C + 20 N/m),
               swung by wind (500 N/m2), against the Swiss legal distances
        |
        v
    encroachment zones (tiered), hazard trees, wire not assessed,
    clearance-coloured point cloud, reports and plots

Phase 3 also uses the Phase 1 vegetation directly (the points it measures).

===============================================================================
7. PHASE 1: CLASSIFYING THE POINT CLOUD
===============================================================================

Goal: give every point one of ground, vegetation, wire, pylon, building.

7.1 Voxel downsampling (io.voxel_downsample)
  The cloud is reduced to one point per 0.15 m cube (the centroid). On t5c2 this
  takes 30.6 M points to 5.5 M. All further work runs on voxels; at the end each
  original point inherits its voxel's label. Rationale: much faster, evens out
  density differences between flight lines, and 0.15 m is fine enough to keep a
  wire (about 3 cm thick) as a thin line of voxels.

7.2 Ground and height above ground (ground.py)
  A progressive morphological filter (Zhang et al., 2003): the lowest point in
  each 0.5 m raster cell; low outliers (cells more than 1 m below their 5 x 5
  median, e.g. multipath "holes") are lifted first so they cannot drag the
  terrain down. Then grey-scale morphological openings with windows growing
  from 3 cells up to 24 m: at each step a cell more than dh above the opened
  surface is not ground, where dh = 0.3 m + 0.3 x (window growth in metres),
  capped at 2.5 m. Small windows remove cars and shrubs, large ones buildings
  and tree clumps, and the growing threshold keeps steep slopes as ground. The
  ground cells are filled and lightly smoothed into a terrain model (DTM). Height
  above ground (HAG) = point z minus the DTM under it. Points below 0.5 m HAG
  are ground.

7.3 Geometric features (features.py)
  For each point, the covariance of its 16 nearest neighbours gives three
  eigenvalues l1 >= l2 >= l3, from which:
    linearity   (l1 - l2) / l1   high on wires (one dominant direction)
    planarity   (l2 - l3) / l1   high on roofs and flat surfaces
    scattering  l3 / l1          high in tree crowns (no structure)
    dir_z       vertical component of the main direction (0 = horizontal)
    normal_z    vertical component of the surface normal
  gap_below: within each 0.5 m column, points are grouped into vertical runs;
  gap_below is the empty space under the run a point belongs to. A wire hangs
  over open air; a branch usually has more of the tree just below it. Using
  runs (not single points) stops a wire's own neighbouring points from closing
  the gap.

7.4 Rules (classify.py; thresholds in Params)
  Wires:
    - HAG > 3 m, linearity >= 0.8, |dir_z| <= 0.6 (main direction within about
      37 degrees of horizontal; raised from a stricter value because steep
      mountain spans failed), gap_below >= 1 m.
    - Connected clusters must be >= 15 m long and >= 4 times longer than wide.
    - Short pieces (>= 3 m) are added back if they line up with a nearby wire
      (direction within about 11 degrees): sparse stretches of a span.
    - Region growing (0.3 m) into linear neighbours fills small gaps.
    - Two sets are kept: all wires and "strict" wires (before growing), used by
      the pylon rule so that tower steel absorbed into wires does not stop a
      tower being found.
  Pylons:
    - Connected clusters >= 10 m tall, median scattering < 0.14 (lattice steel
      is regular; tree crowns scatter around 0.28), whose top is within 3 m of
      a wire and at least as high as the wires there.
    - Points more than 15 m from the tower centre are given back to wire (the
      voxel grid joins wires to the tower they hang from).
  Buildings:
    - Smooth (scattering < 0.05), mostly planar (planarity >= 0.4) clusters of
      >= 100 points and >= 2 m wide.
  Vegetation:
    - Everything else above ground.

7.5 What Phase 1 gets right and wrong (details in section 13)
  - Wires are found reliably: 97-99.5% recall on every tile.
  - Wire precision is lower (70-97%): tower steel and some canopy are called
    wire. Phase 2 is designed to absorb this (validation rejects clusters that
    are not catenaries).
  - Forest towers are often missed: trees as tall as the tower merge with it
    into one cluster that scatters like forest. Phase 2 therefore finds towers
    from the wires instead of relying on Phase 1.
  - Buildings are weak (smooth rock and ground are confused with roofs); they
    play no part in the clearance results.


===============================================================================
8. PHASE 2: REBUILDING THE LINE
===============================================================================

Goal: from the wire points, find the towers, the spans between them, each
individual conductor in each span, and a catenary curve for each conductor.

8.1 Why a graph
  The first version assumed one line per tile: towers sorted along one axis and
  one route cut into spans. At the t2z5 junction (four towers within 60 m, a
  second line leaving) it found 3 of 6 towers and fitted conductors straight
  through the junction. The current version builds a graph: towers are nodes,
  spans are edges, so a tile may hold several lines, branches and junctions.

8.2 Towers: four kinds of evidence, then confirmation
  Candidates from different evidence are merged within 8 m (earlier source
  wins):

  a) Pylon clusters: connected pylon points (>= 200 voxels); pieces within
     10 m merged (one tower can be split by a gap in the scan).

  b) Kinks in the conductor envelope. Wire points are grouped by plan direction
     (peaks of a circular histogram), so each line is handled separately. For
     each group, the upper envelope of the conductors (90th percentile of wire
     z in 1 m steps along the line) must bend downwards: the slope 15 m before
     minus the slope 15 m after > 0.03. A catenary alone always gives a negative
     value (it curves upwards); a tower gives a peak. Both sides must be at
     least 80% covered, so a kink is not invented at the last metres before a
     tile edge (once a tree at wire height there passed as a tower). And the
     line's centre column (2.5 m radius, from 3 m below the lowest conductor to
     1 m below the top wire, searched +-8 m) must hold >= 300 points that are
     neither ground nor wire: at mid-span that column is open air; a tower body
     fills it. True towers had 2,300-7,300 such points, false kinks 0-59.

  c) Direction changes. Wires do not bend in mid-air. Where wire points within
     6 m of each other run in clearly different plan directions (mean resultant
     of doubled angles < 0.9, about 26 degrees), there is an angle or junction
     tower. If every direction carries on past the spot, it is two lines
     crossing, not a tower, and is dropped (t2z5 had one on its main span).

  d) Terminals (second pass). A partial span at least 30 m long that stops
     while the survey continues beyond it (ground beyond, so not a tile edge),
     at structure at wire height, ends on a terminal tower. It must not be
     within 25 m of a known tower (that is a jumper piece), and the structure
     must be steel-shaped: median scattering <= 0.2 (lattice about 0.11, tree
     crowns about 0.28). In t2z5 a line leaving the survey among trees as tall
     as the wires passed every other test.

  Confirmation (after the conductors are fitted): a tower is kept only if a
  fitted conductor hangs from at least one of its spans. On t4z1, canopy that
  Phase 1 called wire (about 86,000 points) kinked and branched like a line and
  produced 4 towers 50-100 m off the real line; none of their spans fitted a
  conductor, so they are now removed (their wire is still reported as
  unmodelled in Phase 3). Tower ids are kept, so removed ones leave gaps.

  Tried and rejected: moving wire-found towers onto the densest "steel" column
  nearby pulled forest towers 8-9 m off, onto the canopy.

  Result: on all seven tiles every true tower is found, within 0.0-2.6 m, and
  none is false.

8.3 Spans (graph edges)
  - Two towers are joined if wire points running along the line between them
    (within 12 m of it) cover at least 60% of its length, in its direction:
    within 20 degrees, or atan(20 m / span length) on short spans. Conductors
    hang from cross-arm ends up to about 10 m either side of a tower centre, so
    on the 15 m span at the t2z5 junction they run at about 50 degrees to the
    line between the tower centres; a fixed 20 degrees missed that span.
  - Bypass pruning: an edge is dropped if a third tower lies within its 12 m
    corridor (the line really goes through that tower), or if it forms a
    triangle of edges with a tower lying within 15% of its length of the line
    (t1z6a's 389 m bypass passed the middle tower at 12.07 m, just outside the
    corridor).
  - Each wire point goes to the nearest edge, only if it lies beside it (not
    beyond its towers, -3 m to L + 3 m along it), aligned, or next to a tower.
  - Wire leaving a tower that no edge explains becomes a partial span (a "ray")
    to where the wire stops; wire near no tower becomes a tower-less span.
  - Spans between two towers are "full"; the rest are "partial".

8.4 Separating the conductors in a span
  - Points within 3 m of a tower are left out first: clamps, insulator strings
    and jumpers hang there and bend away from the catenary.
  - The span's wire points are projected into its cross-section: lateral offset
    from the span line, and height above one quadratic fitted to all of them.
    Parallel conductors keep a constant offset along the span, so in these two
    coordinates each conductor collapses into a compact blob, however patchy it
    is along the span. DBSCAN (eps 0.5 m, 10 points) separates the blobs.
    Bundled sub-conductors (closer than 0.5 m) stay one conductor.

8.5 Fitting a catenary to each conductor
  In the conductor's own vertical plane (u = horizontal distance along it):

      z(u) = z0 + c * (cosh((u - u0) / c) - 1)

  where (u0, z0) is the lowest point of the curve and c = H / w is the
  catenary parameter: horizontal tension H divided by weight per metre w.
  A large c means a tight, flat wire; a small c a slack one. Real conductors
  here have c of about 900-2,400 m (down to about 160 m on short slack spans at
  the junction).

  - Start values come from a parabola fit.
  - Robust least squares (SciPy, soft-L1 loss with a 10 cm scale): residuals
    beyond about 10 cm count linearly, so stray points do not drag the curve.
  - Up to two refits without points more than max(0.5 m, 3 robust sigma)
    away vertically (the residual trim).
  - If the optimiser diverges (on a nearly straight cluster u0 can run off by
    kilometres and cosh overflows inside SciPy), the fit returns NaN and the
    cluster is rejected instead of crashing the run. The frozen code crashed on
    exactly this on held-out tile t4z1.
  - Reported per conductor: c, sag below the chord (full spans), vertical and
    lateral RMSE, coverage, lowest ground clearance, outliers dropped.

8.6 Splitting conductors that were merged
  Two wires at the same height can end up in one cluster: parallel phases
  bridged by stray points, or a wire turning off to another tower. The vertical
  trim cannot see this (both are at the right height), and the fit can still
  look excellent vertically.
  - Sideways split: a real conductor is 2-5 cm wide in plan (lateral RMSE).
    If a fitted conductor's lateral RMSE exceeds 0.3 m, RANSAC (300 random
    pairs, 0.3 m tolerance, pairs at least 5 m apart along the line) finds the
    best-supported straight line in (along, lateral) coordinates. Those points
    are refitted as one conductor; the rest is clustered again and each group
    is fitted in turn. A line in those coordinates handles both parallel wires
    and wires that diverge.
  - Dropped-points refit: if the vertical trim drops at least a fifth of a
    cluster (and at least the minimum point count), the dropped points are
    clustered and fitted too, instead of being silently lost.
  - RMSE re-split: a large cluster rejected for vertical RMSE is re-clustered
    at half the DBSCAN radius (down to 0.125 m) and the pieces refitted (in a
    forest tile, stray tower points had bridged two conductors into one
    6,671-point cluster with a 2.65 m RMSE).
  On t2z5 one 10,416-point cluster held a conductor and a wire diverging from
  it; the fit kept 5,447 points to 6 cm vertically while 4,969 points were
  silently dropped. With these splits, that span is 8 conductors, each 2-3 cm
  wide in plan, and the tile's unmodelled wire fell from 896 m to 235 m.

8.7 Modelling only where the data reaches
  A conductor counts as reaching a tower only if its points come within 8 m of
  it (3 m tower margin + 5 m). If it does not, the model stops where the data
  stops. In t2z5 two wires of a parallel line stopped 53 m short of the span's
  tower; modelled all the way to the tower, they ran through trees and created
  false encroachment zones.

8.8 Validation: which clusters are real conductors
  A cluster is rejected (and listed with its reason in the report) if it:
  - has too few points: 3 per metre of extent, capped at 100; but a piece with
    no tower at either end needs the full 100, since nothing anchors it (on
    t4z1 two scraps of canopy, 64 and 43 points, fitted c = 94 km and 48 m and
    produced a false violation at 0.05 m; real tower-less pieces had 219-318);
  - is shorter than 10 m;
  - covers less than 50% of its extent with points;
  - has c at a fit bound (20 m or 100 km);
  - has a vertical RMSE above 25 cm (real conductors fit to about 2-7 cm);
  - did not converge (non-finite fit);
  - is a piece shorter than 30 m in a tower's span that does not reach the
    tower (wire leaving a tower starts at it; on t1z6a such a 13 m mixture of
    jumper, vegetation and unlabelled points was 0.69 m from a tree and 8 m
    from any real wire).
  This validation is what removes the clusters made of Phase 1's false wire.

8.9 Outputs
  <name>_phase2.json, <name>_conductors.csv, <name>_conductor_model.las (every
  conductor sampled at 0.25 m, the input to Phase 3), plots of points and
  fitted curves per span, and a plan of towers, spans and conductors. When hand
  labels exist, the same Phase 2 is run on hand-labelled wire and each of our
  conductors is matched to that model and compared (height difference, lateral
  difference, sag and c difference).


===============================================================================
9. PHASE 3: CLEARANCE IN EVERY SCENARIO
===============================================================================

Goal: for every vegetation point, the distance to the nearest conductor in each
scenario, compared with the required distance; then zones, hazard trees, and an
honest list of what could not be assessed.

9.1 The legal rules (Swiss Leitungsverordnung, LeV, SR 734.31)
  The lines are in Switzerland, so the defaults follow the LeV for high-voltage
  overhead lines, read from the official text (Fedlex):

  - Art. 35(4)b: vertical distance between trees and bare conductors at
    greatest sag: 1.5 m + 0.01 m per kV (fruit trees 2.5 m + 0.01 m/kV).
    At 380 kV: 5.3 m (at 220 kV: 3.7 m). Applied here as a 3D distance, which
    is stricter beside the line.
  - Art. 47: greatest sag is the worse of (a) the conductor at 40 C and
    (b) the conductor at 0 C with an extra 20 N/m of snow/ice load per
    conductor or sub-conductor, without wind.
  - Definitions no. 37 and Annex 4: wind deflection is computed with the
    conductor at 10 C under 500 N/m2 of wind pressure on the wires.
  - Art. 35(1) leaves the distance to trees in wind case by case; this project
    uses 0.01 m/kV, at least 1.5 m (3.8 m at 380 kV), by analogy with
    Art. 34(2), which sets that distance to the terrain beside the line in wind.

  The nominal voltage is not in the data; 380 kV is assumed (--kv to change).

9.2 The conductor (assumed, a parameter)
  A typical AAAC/Aldrey (AlMgSi alloy) 600 mm2 class conductor, the kind used
  on Swiss lines: diameter 31.9 mm, weight 16.3 N/m, elastic modulus 57 GPa
  (EA about 34 MN), thermal expansion 23e-6 per C, radius 16 mm subtracted
  from axis distances. Not the actual line's datasheet.

9.3 The scenarios
  For each conductor, sampled every 0.1 m over its modelled extent:

  As surveyed
    The Phase 2 catenary: where the wire hung during the flight, assumed at a
    conductor temperature of 15 C (--t-survey).

  Hot (40 C), ice (0 C + 20 N/m) and wind (10 C, 500 N/m2)
    The conductor's horizontal tension at the survey is known from its fitted
    catenary: H1 = c * w. It is carried to the new temperature and load with
    the change-of-state equation for its span (parabolic form):

      H2^2 * [ H2 - H1 + EA*w1^2*L^2 / (24*H1^2) + EA*alpha*(t2 - t1) ]
            = EA * w2^2 * L^2 / 24

    where L is the span length, EA the axial stiffness, alpha the thermal
    expansion coefficient, t1/t2 the temperatures and w1/w2 the loads per
    metre. It balances the wire's elastic stretch against its thermal growth
    and its geometric length: a hotter wire gets longer, sags more, and in
    doing so loses tension, which in turn lets it stretch less ("elastic
    relief"). The new curve has c2 = H2 / w2 and passes through the same two
    attachment points. With an infinitely stiff conductor the equation reduces
    to pure thermal elongation (tested), and solving it forward then back
    returns the starting tension (tested).

    - Hot: w2 = w1 = 16.3 N/m, t2 = 40 C.
    - Ice: w2 = 16.3 + 20 = 36.3 N/m, t2 = 0 C. Cooling from 15 C to 0 C
      shortens the wire and partly cancels the extra load, so on these spans
      the ice case is milder than the hot case. Worked example, a 130 m span:
      2.35 m sag as surveyed, 2.51 m iced, 2.93 m at 40 C (checked by hand
      calculation in the tests).
    - Wind: the load is the resultant of weight and wind,
      wind load = 500 Pa x 0.0319 m = 15.95 N/m, so w2 = sqrt(16.3^2 +
      15.95^2) = 22.8 N/m at 10 C. The loaded curve swings about its chord by
      atan(15.95 / 16.3) = 44.4 degrees, towards either side ("blowout left"
      and "blowout right").

  Attachment points (where the curve is pinned) come from what each
  conductor's data reaches:
    - both towers: exact;
    - one tower and at least 30 m of data: the far attachment is extrapolated
      along the fitted curve at the median full-span length of the tile;
      flagged "estimated";
    - otherwise (a short piece, or no tower): checked as surveyed only, and
      counted in conductors_not_assessed_for_sag_wind.
    Extrapolating 15 m pieces over a 270 m span had swung them metres sideways
    in the wind case, into trees nowhere near the real wire.

9.4 Cleaning the vegetation first
  Phase 1 errors would otherwise become false alarms, so three kinds of
  "vegetation" are removed before measuring:
  - Near towers: vegetation-labelled points within 8 m (in plan) of a tower
    centre. On t1z6a the only 3 false zones were hand-labelled tower steel
    3-6 m from a tower centre, while every real zone was at least 30 m from a
    tower. The cost: a tree inside a tower's footprint is not reported.
  - Floating fragments: vegetation clusters (1 m connectivity) with fewer than
    200 points and nothing below 3 m above ground. Plants grow from the ground;
    every false zone these caused on the alpine tiles was hand-labelled
    conductor.
  - On-conductor points: within 0.5 m of the conductor model with at least 80%
    of their 1 m neighbourhood also on it (a piece of wire). A crown touching
    the wire keeps its contact points, because the crown behind them is in
    their neighbourhood (tested).

9.5 Clearance and tiers
  For each vegetation point and scenario: the 3D distance to the nearest
  conductor axis minus the conductor radius. Each scenario has its required
  distance (5.3 m hot/ice and as surveyed, 3.8 m wind). The scenario with the
  smallest margin (clearance minus required distance) governs that point.
  Tiers by margin:
      violation   margin < 0               inside the legal distance
      urgent      0 <= margin < 1.5 m      will be inside it soon; trim now
      monitor     1.5 m <= margin < 3 m    within 3 years of growth (1 m/year
                                           x 3-year cycle, both assumptions)
      clear       margin >= 3 m

9.6 Encroachment zones
  Flagged points (violation, urgent, monitor) are clustered with 1 m
  connectivity (at least 5 points). For each zone: tier, governing scenario,
  clearance, required distance and margin, the minimum clearance in every
  scenario, nearest span and conductor, horizontal and vertical offset from the
  conductor, location, point count, plan area, years to violation, and whether
  it relies on an estimated attachment. Sorted worst first. Written as CSV and
  as GeoJSON (zone outlines in the tile's projected coordinates).

9.7 Unmodelled wire (what was not assessed)
  Clearance can only be measured to modelled conductors. Wire-labelled points
  more than 1 m from every conductor model, grouped into line-shaped stretches
  (at least 10 m long, 4 times longer than wide, away from towers), are listed
  with their length and the number of vegetation points within the watch
  distance of each, as NOT ASSESSED, instead of silently counting that
  vegetation as clear.

9.8 Hazard (fall-in) trees
  A tree outside the clearance zone can still hit the line when it falls.
  - Treetops: local maxima (3 m window) of a 0.5 m canopy height model, at
    least 3 m tall.
  - Each vegetation point is assigned to its nearest treetop (within
    0.4 x height + 2 m). From its points each tree gets a crown radius (spread
    of the upper half) and a base (where its low points stand, if there are at
    least 10; otherwise straight below the top), so a leaning tree pivots at
    its real base.
  - Reach = sqrt(stem length^2 + crown radius^2) from the base, in any
    direction (a sphere, so falls down a slope are included).
  - Status: "strike" if the reach touches a conductor in any scenario;
    "within_mvcd" if it comes within the wind distance (3.8 m); otherwise safe.
  - Not modelled: which way a leaning tree will actually fall.

9.9 Outputs
  <name>_phase3.json (everything, including scores), _encroachments.csv and
  .geojson, _hazard_trees.csv, _unmodelled_wire.csv,
  _vegetation_clearance.las (each vegetation point with fields "clearance" and
  "tier"), and plots: an along-line clearance profile, a plan of zones and
  hazard trees, and a cross-section at the worst zone showing every conductor
  as surveyed, hot, iced and swung, with the required-distance circles.

===============================================================================
11. FINDING TILES WORTH DOWNLOADING (REMOTE SCREENING)
===============================================================================

The first two tiles had no trees near the line, so Phase 3 could not be
checked on real encroachments. Downloading every 2 GB tile to look would be
slow, so sample.py reads the LAS header and then about 2% of the points as
2,000 evenly spaced chunks, by HTTP byte range (Hugging Face honours Range
requests): about 40 MB and 2.5-6 minutes per zone instead of gigabytes.
screen_zones.py then measures how close hand-labelled vegetation comes to
hand-labelled wire.

  - Chunk count matters more than sample size, because points are not stored
    in a spatially uniform order: 300 chunks put t5b6's closest vegetation at
    19.7 m, 2,000-3,000 chunks at 8.9-9.2 m (full file: 8.4 m).
  - 16 of the 27 labelled zones were screened. Closest vegetation to a wire
    (sample): t1z6a 1.7 m, t1z6b 2.5 m, t1z8 3.0 m, t2z5 3.3 m, t4z1 3.7 m,
    t1z5b 4.2 m, t6z2 and t6z3 5.3 m, others 8-52 m. The forest tiles and the
    junction were chosen from this list.


===============================================================================
12. HOW THE RESULTS ARE SCORED
===============================================================================

12.1 Phase 1: per-class precision, recall and IoU (intersection over union)
  against the hand labels, on every original point, plus a confusion matrix.

12.2 Phase 2: the same Phase 2 is run on the hand-labelled wire (and
  hand-labelled pylons). Each of our conductors is matched to that model and
  compared: towers found, conductor counts, height difference along the curve
  (median and maximum), vertical RMSE.

12.3 Phase 3, end to end (the headline score)
  The reference is a run built entirely from the hand labels: a conductor model
  fitted to the hand-labelled wire, and the hand-labelled vegetation, with the
  same rules and scenarios. Our run is compared with it:
    - flagged points: by index (same voxels), precision and recall;
    - zones: a predicted zone matches a reference zone if they share a point;
      precision, recall, and whether matched pairs have the same tier;
    - hazard trees: matched if within 2 m in plan.
  This covers both classification and conductor modelling errors. It does not
  test the sag and wind assumptions themselves (both runs share them).

12.4 Phase 3, same model (in the reports)
  Our conductor model with the hand-labelled vegetation. It isolates Phase 1's
  vegetation errors but is blind to a wrong conductor model: on t2z5 before
  the graph it showed 90% / 100% zones while end to end was 77% / 82%. That is
  why the end-to-end score is the one reported.

12.5 Held-out protocol
  A held-out tile is run once, on frozen code (a fixed commit exported to a
  separate folder), before the tile has been looked at, and the result is
  recorded as it comes out, good or bad. After that the tile may be used to
  find and fix problems, but it no longer counts as held out.

===============================================================================
14. HOW THE PROJECT DEVELOPED: PROBLEMS FOUND AND HOW EACH WAS FIXED
===============================================================================

Each phase was built, run on real labelled data, and the failures traced to
their cause. In order:

  Phase 1
  - Truth labels were being read as raw GridNet ids rather than grouped
    classes: fixed the mapping.
  - Steep mountain spans failed the wire rules: the direction limit was relaxed
    (dir_z 0.6) and gap_below changed to use vertical runs, so a wire's own
    neighbours no longer close its air gap.
  - Tower steel absorbed into wires stopped towers being found: pylon detection
    now uses the "strict" wires (before region growing).

  Phase 2
  - Tower hardware spoiled the fits: 3 m tower margin, residual trim and the
    validation rules.
  - Forest towers fused with the canopy (0 towers found on t1z6a): a per-point
    scattering filter was tried and rejected (it broke the alpine towers);
    towers are now found from wire kinks plus the centre-column test.
  - Two conductors bridged into one (2.65 m RMSE): re-split on RMSE failure.
  - Wires stopping short of a tower were modelled through the trees: model only
    where the data reaches.
  - Moving towers onto "steel" pulled forest towers 8-9 m off: removed.
  - The single-route model failed at the t2z5 junction: rebuilt as a graph.
  - A bypass edge passing a tower at 12.07 m: triangle rule added.
  - A false angle tower where two lines cross: crossing rejection.
  - False terminals (short rays, then trees at wire height): minimum ray 30 m,
    25 m separation from known towers, then the steel-shape test.
  - A false tower from a one-sided kink at a tile edge: 80% coverage rule.
  - Merge distance too large merged junction towers: 8 m.
  - A 13 m piece of jumper and vegetation 0.69 m from a tree: the "short piece
    detached from its tower" rule.
  - A missed 15 m junction span: angle tolerance atan(20 m / L).
  - t2z5's 896 m of unmodelled wire: merged and diverging conductors; the
    dropped-points refit and the RANSAC sideways split (896 -> 235 m).
  - t4z1 (held out) crashed on a diverging fit: fit returns NaN, rejected.
  - t4z1 false towers in canopy labelled wire: tower confirmation.
  - t4z1 false violation from canopy scraps: full point count for tower-less
    pieces.

  Phase 3
  - False zones from tower steel labelled vegetation: 8 m tower exclusion.
  - False zones from conductor points labelled vegetation: floating-fragment
    and on-conductor cleaning (keeping real contact points).
  - Short pieces extrapolated across whole spans swung into trees in wind: the
    attachment rule (exact / estimated / not assessed).
  - Placeholder 3 m thresholds replaced with the Swiss LeV rules, and a simple
    sag scaling replaced with the change-of-state equation, the ice case and
    the wind case.
  - Vegetation beside unmodelled wire silently counted as clear: the
    unmodelled-wire report.
  - Hazard trees as vertical sticks: crown radius and lean (base) added.
  - Spans cut by tile edges: tile joining.

  Process
  - Remote screening was needed to find tiles with trees near the line; 300
    sampled chunks were too few, 2,000 were enough.
  - Large downloads dropped mid-way: resumed with curl -C - and a size check.


===============================================================================
15. ASSUMPTIONS AND LIMITATIONS
===============================================================================

  Rules and inputs
  - The voltage (380 kV), conductor type, survey temperature (15 C) and maximum
    operating temperature (40 C) are assumed, not read from the line's records.
    All are parameters.
  - Growth (1 m/year) and the trimming cycle (3 years), which define the
    monitor tier, are assumptions.
  - Art. 35 sets a vertical distance; it is applied as a 3D distance.
  - The wind-case tree distance (3.8 m) is by analogy with Art. 34(2), since
    Art. 35(1) leaves it case by case.

  Physics
  - Sag-tension is parabolic, per actual span: no ruling span (in reality
    suspension insulators let tension equalise between spans), no creep, no
    insulator swing.
  - One survey temperature for the whole flight.
  - Bundled conductors would be modelled at the bundle centre (these lines
    show single conductors).

  Geometry and coverage
  - Partial spans: the model is valid only over the stretch with points; c is
    weakly determined; far attachments are estimated (joining tiles helps).
  - A terminal tower within 25-30 m of the previous tower is not found.
  - Several parallel lines sharing towers are not told apart.
  - A tree within 8 m of a tower centre is not reported (tower exclusion).
  - Hazard trees: the fall direction of a leaning tree is not modelled; reach
    is a sphere.

  Classification
  - Buildings are poorly classified (no effect on clearance).
  - Forest towers are often missed by Phase 1 (Phase 2 compensates).
  - Canopy can be called wire; Phase 2 rejects it, but it then appears as
    "unmodelled wire, not assessed" (a cautious false alarm).

  Validation
  - The end-to-end reference is this project's own Phase 2 on hand-labelled
    points; at junctions it is itself imperfect.
  - Only two tiles were truly held out (t1z8 passed, t4z1 crashed). The other
    numbers come from tiles used during development.
  - All data is from Swiss lines of one dataset; other sensors, densities and
    line types are untested.

18. GLOSSARY
===============================================================================

  AAAC          All-aluminium-alloy conductor (here AlMgSi, "Aldrey").
  ASPRS codes   Standard LAS classification numbers (2 ground, 5 high
                vegetation, 6 building, 14 wire-conductor, 15 transmission
                tower).
  Attachment    Where a conductor is held at a tower (insulator clamp).
  Blowout       Sideways swing of a conductor in wind.
  c             Catenary parameter, H / w, in metres; larger = tighter.
  Catenary      The curve a hanging flexible cable takes under its own weight.
  Change-of-state equation  Relates a conductor's tension at one temperature
                and load to another, accounting for elastic stretch and
                thermal growth.
  Conductor     One wire carrying current (a phase), or an earth wire.
  DBSCAN        Density-based clustering: groups points that are within eps
                of enough neighbours.
  DTM           Digital terrain model: the bare-ground surface.
  Eigenvalue features  Shape descriptors from the covariance of a point's
                neighbours (linear, planar, scattered).
  Encroachment zone  A connected group of vegetation points too close to a
                conductor in some scenario.
  End to end    Scored against a reference built entirely from hand labels.
  Full span     A span between two found towers (else partial).
  HAG           Height above ground.
  Hazard tree   A tree that could reach a conductor if it fell.
  Held out      A tile run once on frozen code before being looked at.
  IoU           Intersection over union: overlap of predicted and true sets.
  LAS           The standard file format for LiDAR point clouds.
  LeV           Leitungsverordnung, the Swiss ordinance on electrical lines
                (SR 734.31).
  LiDAR         Laser scanning; measures 3D points by timing laser pulses.
  Margin        Clearance minus required distance; negative = violation.
  MVCD          Minimum vegetation clearance distance (the required distance).
  PMF           Progressive morphological filter (ground filtering).
  RANSAC        Random sample consensus: fits a model to the largest subset of
                points that agrees with it, ignoring the rest.
  RMSE          Root mean square error of a fit.
  Sag           Vertical distance between the chord (straight line between
                attachments) and the conductor.
  Span          The stretch of line between two towers.
  Tier          Violation / urgent / monitor / clear.
  Voxel         A small cube; voxel downsampling keeps one point per cube.
